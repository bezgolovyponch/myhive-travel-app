# Imagery style and licensing for a stag / group-experience catalogue (Prague)

All pages fetched 2026-10-10 unless stated. Where a site blocked the fetch (HTTP 403) that is recorded in Gaps rather than guessed. Alt-text and image-URL dimensions are what the live HTML exposed; the rendered pixels themselves were not inspected, so "what the card shows" is inferred from alt text / filenames and labelled as such.

## KQ1. Photo conventions on stag/bachelor and experience marketplaces, and evidence that image choices move bookings

### Takeaway
UK stag sites (LNOF, Chillisauce, Pissup) run small fixed-crop landscape or square thumbnails with no price on the image, alt text = activity name, and lean on explicit "girls" imagery for adult products; the mainstream marketplaces (GetYourGuide, Airbnb) prescribe the opposite discipline: one hero photo per listing, people visibly doing the activity, wide shot, no text/watermarks, natural light, a fixed ratio (GYG 4:3 horizontal, Airbnb 2:3 vertical). Evidence that photos move bookings exists but is all first-party/observational (Airbnb +19–20% bookings, Expedia +63% CTR, GYG "55% of travellers rank photos among the most helpful factors"); no A/B evidence specific to stag catalogues was found.

- Expedia Group: "hotel listings with high-quality photos have a 63% higher click-through rate than those without. And having just one photo, as opposed to none, increases the chances of booking by 225%" — [Expedia Group DIY hotel photography](https://welcome.expediagroup.com/en/resources/hotel-marketing-strategy-resources-tips/do-it-yourself-hotel-photography); the same 63% is quoted elsewhere as "more bookings" and the 225% is attributed to Tripadvisor "booking inquiry", so the metrics are inconsistent across sources — [Triptease deck](https://www.triptease.com/hubfs/2026/Webinar/Onsite%20storytelling%20that%20converts%20%E2%80%94%20webinar%20deck.pdf)
- Expedia UX-lab (electromyography) finding: "photographs which are distorted by fisheye lenses or unusual angles evoke the most negative emotions in shoppers"; room-with-landmark-view images evoked most delight — [Travolution](https://www.travolution.com/articles/12115/expedia-study-finds-that-quality-photos-boost-ota-sales); [Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com.au/article/rooms-with-views-sell-expedia/)

- Baymard PLP research: the thumbnail is "a primary tool users rely on to decide whether to explore or ignore an item"; "if a visual is missing, misleading, or lacks sufficient information, users frequently overlook suitable products"; "Provide thumbnails for every item"; guideline #443 is titled "Include Product Attributes Consistently Across List Items" (title only, details paywalled) — [Baymard PLP UX](https://baymard.com/blog/product-listing-page-plp-ux)
- NN/g 2006 eyetracking (second-hand): task-focused users largely ignore images; faces attract fixation; ad-like graphics are tuned out — [Poynter](https://www.poynter.org/news/what-makes-web-images-attractive)

**Consistency rules from design systems / brand guides**
- "Art direction is the set of constraints that makes 100 different photographs feel like they belong to the same product" — pin down subject matter, colour temperature, contrast, depth of field, composition, whether people appear, before sourcing — [design-imagery-photography skill](https://openskillindex.com/skills/intense-visions-harness-engineering-design-imagery-photography-1cc7b2)
- Forrester design system: "Images are more inviting and relatable with lighting that feels natural", high dynamic range, natural white balance rather than stylised colour, no cluttered compositions or contrived poses; stock allowed except for team photos — [Forrester photography](https://go.forrester.com/brand/design-system/photography/)
- Frequency Media brand: "Natural, soft lighting preferred. Avoid harsh shadows or overly dramatic lighting."; slightly desaturated, cool treatment tied to the brand blue; uncluttered frames with room for overlay text — [Frequency Media imagery](https://brand.frequency.media/guidelines/imagery)
- Pavilion brand: rule-of-thirds placement of people, shallow depth of field, room for graphic overlays — [Pavilion photography](https://brand.joinpavilion.com/design/photography)
- Unisco: choose a small fixed set of aspect ratios rather than per image; sRGB for digital — [Unisco photography](https://design.unisco.com/guidelines/photography)
- UK Home Office design system: a single consistent illustration style builds trust; use images only when they help users — [Home Office images](https://design.homeoffice.gov.uk/design-system/styles/images)

### Inferences
- The grid "feels irritating" on UK stag sites mainly because of mixed sources with no shared treatment (operator snapshots, B&W, split images, stock girls) inside an otherwise rigid crop; the crop is consistent, the colour and subject are not. Chillisauce (fixed fill-crop + alt pattern + badges in HTML, not on the image) is the cleanest of the three.
- The two things every mainstream platform converges on are (a) one fixed landscape ratio per card (GYG 4:3; Pissup 270×210 is 9:7, close to 4:3) and (b) the price/badges live in the card chrome, never burned into the image. 1200×900 (4:3) satisfies GYG's minimum and matches the Pissup/Chillisauce card shape; Airbnb's vertical 2:3 is the outlier and only matters if you syndicate there.
- The conversion evidence supports "people visibly doing this activity, wide, natural light, no text" over venue-only or equipment-only shots, but all of it is first-party and observational; treat the exact percentages as directional.

### Gaps
- GetYourGuide's live Prague listing page (`getyourguide.com/prague-l10/`) returned HTTP 403, so its current card ratio, badges ("Bestseller", wishlist heart) and image treatment could not be confirmed first-hand; the supplier guide (4:3, hero rules) is the proxy.
- Chillisauce's "Spicy" images are gated behind an enquiry form; what they look like is unknown.
- No Baymard, NN/g, or marketplace A/B test specifically about experience/tour thumbnails (vs. hotel rooms or e-commerce products) was found. No Airbnb Design-blog post on Experiences photography surfaced.
- Rendered colour grading / saturation of the stag sites was not measured (text-only fetch); descriptions of "neon/dark/warm" would be speculation.

## KQ2. Legal position on reusing competitor websites' photos (CZ / EU / UK / US), hotlinking, and "fair use" myths

### Takeaway
Copying a competitor's photo and re-hosting it is infringement in all four regimes unless licensed; in the EU the CJEU (Renckhoff) says re-posting a freely available photo is itself a new "communication to the public" requiring consent, and Czech law offers no commercial exception (§30 personal use is non-commercial only). Hotlinking is legally murkier (US "server test" upheld in 2023; EU: lawful only absent anti-framing measures per VG Bild-Kunst) but is forbidden by most stock-site terms and is operationally fragile, so it is not a sourcing strategy either.

### Cited Findings
- UK IPO notice "Copyright notice: digital images, photographs and the internet" (updated 4 Jan 2021): "The vast majority of images on the internet are likely to be protected by copyright"; "Copying images and then hosting them on another website however will usually amount to copyright infringement"; sharing "a simple web link is generally not restricted" but only where the rights holder published the material; term is "the life of the creator plus 70 years from the end of the calendar year of their death"; "some exceptions are not available for photographs"; "Be aware that some Creative Commons licences are for non-commercial use only"; "Copyright does not disappear simply because the owner cannot be found" (orphan-works licence route); infringers "could also be asked to take down and permanently remove all copies" and "Deliberate infringement of copyright on a commercial scale may also lead to a criminal prosecution" — [GOV.UK IPO](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet)
- US fair use (17 U.S.C. §107, copyright.gov): four factors — "Purpose and character of the use, including whether the use is of a commercial nature", "Nature of the copyrighted work", "Amount and substantiality of the portion used", "Effect of the use upon the potential market"; "Courts evaluate fair use claims on a case-by-case basis"; "Transformative uses are those that add something new, with a further purpose or different character" — [copyright.gov Fair Use](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)
- US embedding/hotlinking: the Ninth Circuit in Hunley v. Instagram (17 Jul 2023, No. 22-15293) "reaffirmed the server test, which shields websites from copyright liability for embedding images hosted on another website's server", holding Perfect 10 v. Amazon controls; "Several district courts outside the Ninth Circuit have rejected the rule" and commentators expect further review — [DWT](https://dwt.com/insights/2023/08/server-test-instagram-case-ninth-circuit); [Copyright Lately](https://copyrightlately.com/ninth-circuit-affirms-server-test-in-instagram-embedding-case)
- EU re-posting: CJEU C-161/17 Renckhoff (2018) — posting a photo on a website "amounted to a communication to the public, even where the photograph had previously been published on another website with the consent of the copyright owner"; re-uploading "requires authorisation, even where the original was freely available" — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/copyright/734678/copyright-and-freely-accessible-photos-the-implications-for-website-operators-and-social-media-users); [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/services/intellectual-property/intellectual-property-blog/school-taught-a-lesson-on-communicating-copyright-works)
- EU linking: Svensson — a hyperlink to a work already freely available reaches no "new public" and is not infringing — [dandi.media summary](https://www.dandi.media/en/european-court-justice-hyperlink/)
- EU framing/inline embedding: CJEU Grand Chamber C-392/19 VG Bild-Kunst (9 Mar 2021) — embedding by framing "is a communication to the public where it circumvents protection measures against framing that the rightholder adopted or imposed"; without such measures the rightholder is treated as having authorised access to all internet users; rightholders must rely on effective technological measures, not contract alone; resizing is irrelevant "provided its original elements remain perceptible". The Advocate General had distinguished inline linking (requires authorisation) from framing (does not), but the Court's test turns on technical measures — [CURIA press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/jcms/p1_3453439); [Wiggin](https://wiggin.co.uk/?p=16875); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2021/03/15/copyright-holders-can-restrict-framing-says-cjeu/id=130930/); [Pérez-Llorca briefing](https://perezllorca.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/legal-briefing-the-cjeu-rules-on-the-embedding-of-third-party-works-in-a-website-by-means-of-the-technique-of-framing.pdf)
- Czech Copyright Act (Act No. 121/2000 Coll., Czech text as amended to 50/2019 on WIPO Lex; latest consolidation to Act 429/2022 at entry 22298): §2(2) "Za dílo se považuje též počítačový program, fotografie a výtvor vyjádřený postupem podobným fotografii" (photographs are works); §12(1) "Autor má právo své dílo užít…" (author's exclusive right to use and to authorise); §27(1) "Majetková práva trvají … po dobu autorova života a 70 let po jeho smrti"; §29(1) exceptions only "ve zvláštních případech stanovených zákonem" (three-step test); §30(1) personal use of a natural person without commercial benefit is not a "use"; §31(1)(a) quotation "v odůvodněné míře"; §40(1) remedies include prohibition, removal of effects and compensation for non-material harm — [WIPO Lex CZ 19501](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/19501); [WIPO Lex CZ 22298 (current)](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/22298)
- Czechia protects photographs only when original (own intellectual creation) and "did not implement Article 14 as non-original reproductions are not protected under Czech copyright law" — [Europeana Czechia rights page](https://europeana.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EF/pages/2406056109/Czechia)
- Czech §33 (freedom of panorama) permits photographing/reusing works permanently located in a public place, per Wikimedia Commons' quotation of the statute (not verified against the official text) — [search summary citing Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia)
- Stock sites themselves restrict hotlinking: Pixabay "permanent hotlinking of images (using Pixabay URLs in your app) is not allowed" — "download them to your server first" — [Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/); Wikimedia Commons: hotlinking "is also possible, but is not recommended" — [Commons reuse](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia); Unsplash API apps, conversely, "must use the hotlinked image URLs returned by the API" — [Unsplash API guidelines](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2511245-unsplash-api-guidelines)

### Inferences
- "It was on the internet / had no © notice / we're linking not copying / it's fair use" are all wrong for a commercial Prague catalogue: the UK and Czech regimes have no general fair-use defence (only enumerated exceptions, some unavailable for photos), and even under US §107 a whole photo reused for the same commercial purpose (selling the same activity) fails factors 1, 3 and 4.
- Hotlinking a competitor's image from an EU-hosted site is at best "not infringing until they add a referrer check / token" (VG Bild-Kunst), and they can swap the file for anything at any time; it also exposes your users' IPs to the competitor. Not viable.
- Stag operators' photos are usually operator-supplied or agency-licensed; the right route for venue-specific shots is to ask the Prague supplier for their own press/partner images under a written licence (not researched here, see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No English consolidated text of the current Czech act (as amended to 429/2022) is on WIPO Lex; §§ quoted are from the Czech text of the 2019 consolidation. The §33 freedom-of-panorama wording was only seen via Wikimedia.
- No Czech case law on commercial reuse of web photos was found (only two theses: [VŠE 73072](https://vskp.vse.cz/english/73072), [CUNI thesis](https://dspace.cuni.cz/handle/20.500.11956/86048)).
- Whether an en banc rehearing or Supreme Court review of Hunley has happened since 2023 was not checked.
- Competitor websites' own terms of use (contractual bar on scraping images) were not fetched.

## KQ3. Licence-safe image sources: what each licence allows for a commercial catalogue, identifiable-people caveats, and the direct-URL / API mechanics for a one-off download-and-rehost script

### Takeaway
Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay all permit free commercial use without attribution, but none warrants model releases and all three carve out "recognisable people in a bad light / immoral / offensive" uses; Pixabay explicitly requires you to download rather than hotlink, Unsplash's API guidelines require hotlinking only for apps that serve images via the API (the licence itself allows download/copy/modify). Wikimedia Commons is CC BY/BY-SA (attribution mandatory, BY-SA makes graded derivatives share-alike) and Openverse is only an index pointing at Flickr etc. Freepik's free tier requires visible "Designed by Freepik" credit. All sources expose stable, parameterisable URLs that a server can fetch once.

### Cited Findings

**Unsplash**
- Licence: "All images can be downloaded and used for free", "Commercial and non-commercial purposes", "No permission needed (though attribution is appreciated!)"; grant "to download, copy, modify, distribute … for free, including for commercial purposes, without permission from or attributing the photographer or Unsplash"; prohibited: "Images cannot be sold without significant modification" and "This license does not include the right to compile images from Unsplash to replicate a similar or competing service"; no date on the page — [unsplash.com/license](https://unsplash.com/license)
- Terms §5 lists what the licence does not cover: "People's images if they are recognizable in the Images" and "Trademarks, logos, or brands that appear in Images"; service "AS-IS … WITHOUT ANY WARRANTY … NON-INFRINGEMENT" (§15) — [unsplash.com/terms](https://unsplash.com/terms)
- Releases: "contributors agree that images uploaded to Unsplash have model releases" but "We cannot make any guarantees about the scope of permitted uses"; advice is to "reach out to the contributor to clarify whether they have a model or property release"; a branded t-shirt in frame "may have copyright or trademark protection" — [Unsplash help: Releases and trademark](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612329-releases-and-trademark); Unsplash's contributor guidance says a release is required "for each recognizable person, regardless of whether they are the main subject … or in the background" — [Unsplash help 6604664](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/6604664-when-do-i-need-to-get-a-model-release)
- API (docs as fetched 2026-10-10): "For applications in demo mode, the Unsplash API currently places a limit of 50 requests per hour. After approval for production, this limit is increased to 1000 requests per hour." Headers "X-Ratelimit-Limit: 1000 / X-Ratelimit-Remaining: 999"; "only the json requests (i.e., those to api.unsplash.com) are counted. Image file requests (images.unsplash.com) do not count against your rate limit." `urls` object: `raw` (base + ixid, for building custom URLs), `full` (max-size JPG, discouraged), `regular` (1080 px wide), `small` (400), `thumb` (200). Supported Imgix params: `w`, `h`, `crop`, `fm`, `auto=format`, `q`, `fit`, `dpr`; "All resizing and manipulations of image URLs must keep this [ixid] parameter". Search params: `orientation` = landscape | portrait | squarish; `content_filter` = low (default, excludes nudity/violence) | high; download tracking `GET /photos/:id/download` — [Unsplash API docs](https://unsplash.com/documentation)
- API guidelines: "All API uses must use the hotlinked image URLs returned by the API under the photo.urls properties"; "you must send a request to the download endpoint returned under the photo.links.download_location property"; must "attribute Unsplash, the Unsplash photographer, and contain a link back to their Unsplash profile" with `?utm_source=your_app_name&utm_medium=referral`; "You cannot replicate the core user experience of Unsplash" — [Unsplash API guidelines](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2511245-unsplash-api-guidelines)
- Direct URL shape (from the docs): `https://images.unsplash.com/photo-<id>?ixid=<…>&w=1200&h=900&fit=crop&crop=entropy&q=80&fm=jpg` — [Unsplash API docs](https://unsplash.com/documentation)

**Pexels**
- Licence: "All photos and videos on Pexels are free to use."; "Attribution is not required."; "You can modify the photos and videos"; not allowed: "Identifiable people may not appear in a bad light or in a way that is offensive."; "Don't sell unaltered copies"; "Don't imply endorsement of your product by people or brands on the imagery."; no use "as part of your trade-mark … business name"; no redistribution on other stock platforms; model releases not mentioned — [pexels.com/license](https://www.pexels.com/license/)
- API: "200 requests per hour and 20,000 per month" by default; "Whenever you are doing an API request make sure to show a prominent link to Pexels"; credit photographers "when possible" ("Photo by John Doe on Pexels"); "You may not copy or replicate core functionality of Pexels"; no hotlinking or caching rule in the docs. `src` sizes: `original`; `large` 940×650 @1x; `large2x` 940×650 @2x; `medium` height 350; `small` height 130; `portrait` 800×1200 crop; `landscape` 1200×627 crop; `tiny` 280×200 crop — [Pexels API docs](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/)

## Practical recipe: make ~40 mixed-source images read as one set (4:3, 1200×900, JPEG q80)

### Takeaway
Fix one ratio (4:3), one output size (1200×900), one grade (slight desaturation, a touch of warmth, mild contrast, normalised levels), strip metadata, and keep any brand gradient/text in CSS on the card rather than baked into the file. Both Sharp and ImageMagick do this in one pass.

### Cited Findings
- Sharp one-pass (all calls from the official API docs cited in KQ3):

```js
// node harmonise.mjs <in-dir> <out-dir>
import sharp from 'sharp';
import { readdir } from 'node:fs/promises';
import path from 'node:path';

const [inDir, outDir] = process.argv.slice(2);
for (const f of await readdir(inDir)) {
  await sharp(path.join(inDir, f))
    .rotate()                                   // honour EXIF orientation
    .resize(1200, 900, { fit: 'cover', position: 'attention' }) // 4:3 smart crop
    .normalise({ lower: 1, upper: 99 })         // consistent black/white points
    .modulate({ brightness: 1.0, saturation: 0.85, hue: 0 })    // slight desaturation
    .linear(1.06, -6)                           // mild contrast: 1.06*x - 6
    .tint({ r: 255, g: 244, b: 230 })           // faint warm cast (toward brand warmth)
    .jpeg({ quality: 80, mozjpeg: true, chromaSubsampling: '4:2:0' })
    .toFile(path.join(outDir, f.replace(/\.[^.]+$/, '.jpg')));  // no metadata kept by default
}
```
  `fit:'cover'` + `position:'attention'` = the documented replacement for `crop('attention')`; `modulate` saturation 1 = unchanged; `linear(a,b)` = `a*input+b`; `normalise` percentiles default 1/99; `jpeg` quality default 80, `mozjpeg:true` = trellis + overshoot deringing + optimise scans — [sharp docs](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/api-operation)
- ImageMagick equivalent (fill-crop idiom and `-modulate`/`-colorize`/`-auto-level` quoted from the docs; `-sigmoidal-contrast`, `-strip`, `-quality` not re-verified this session):

```sh
magick in.jpg -auto-orient \
  -resize 1200x900^ -gravity center -extent 1200x900 \
  -auto-level -modulate 100,85,100 \
  -fill '#ffb347' -colorize 5 \
  -sigmoidal-contrast 3x50% \
  -strip -sampling-factor 4:2:0 -quality 80 out.jpg
# batch, in place into out/:
mogrify -path out/ -auto-orient -resize 1200x900^ -gravity center -extent 1200x900 \
  -auto-level -modulate 100,85,100 -fill '#ffb347' -colorize 5 \
  -sigmoidal-contrast 3x50% -strip -quality 80 *.jpg
```
  `^` = "resize the image based on the smallest fitting dimension" then `-gravity center -extent` crops to exact size; `-modulate 100,85,100` = brightness unchanged, saturation 85 %, hue unchanged; `-colorize 5` blends 5 % of the `-fill` colour — [ImageMagick Usage: Resize](https://usage.imagemagick.org/resize/); [ImageMagick options](https://imagemagick.org/command-line-options/)
- Why 4:3 landscape and no text: GetYourGuide "horizontal/landscape, 4:3", "No watermarks or text overlays"; Google Things-to-do "avoid overlays and heavy filters"; Pissup cards are 270×210; Chillisauce fixed fill-crop at 290 px — [GetYourGuide Growth Hub](https://www.getyourguide.supply/articles/your-first-photo-can-make-or-break-your-bookings); [Pissup Prague](https://www.pissup.com/prague-stag-do/); [Chillisauce Prague](https://www.chillisauce.com/stag-weekends/prague)
- Why one grade: brand guides specify a single treatment ("slightly desaturated", "natural white balance", "soft lighting", shallow DoF) so sourced photos "feel like they belong to the same product" — [Frequency Media](https://brand.frequency.media/guidelines/imagery); [Forrester](https://go.forrester.com/brand/design-system/photography/); [art-direction checklist](https://openskillindex.com/skills/intense-visions-harness-engineering-design-imagery-photography-1cc7b2)
- Fetching politely: Wikimedia requires an informative User-Agent with contact info or returns 403; Pixabay webformat URLs expire after 24 h and API responses must be cached 24 h; Unsplash image-file requests don't count against the API limit — [WMF UA policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Wikimedia_Foundation_User-Agent_Policy); [Pixabay API](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/); [Unsplash API](https://unsplash.com/documentation)

### Inferences
- Selection rules do more than the grade: pick daylight or evenly lit club shots, one dominant subject at roughly the same scale, horizon near the lower third, no in-frame text/logos (brands also void the licence for commercial use on Pixabay/Unsplash), and avoid black-and-white and split images (LNOF's mix is what makes its grid look assembled). Run the Sharp pass, then eyeball the 40 as a contact sheet and swap the two or three that still fight the set.
- Keep the brand gradient overlay and the price/badge in the card CSS (as Chillisauce and letsbatch do in HTML) so the same JPEG serves the listing, the OG image and ads, and so a re-brand doesn't mean re-processing.
- Output both 1200×900 (card/OG) and 600×450 (grid thumbnail, `w=600` on the fly from R2 via Next `<Image>` is fine) and keep the 2000-px source in R2 for later re-grading.

### Gaps
- `position: 'attention'` crops toward luminance/colour saliency; for shots with a person off-centre it may still cut heads at 4:3 from a portrait source — no documented accuracy figures; manual review of the 40 is the mitigation.
- No source compared JPEG q80 vs WebP/AVIF for travel thumbnails; Sharp supports `.webp()`/`.avif()` if the frontend wants them.
