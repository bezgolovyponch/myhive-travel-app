# Evidence-based UX of activity/experience cards and optional add-on presentation (e-commerce + travel booking, as of 2026-10-10)

Scope note: findings below come from (a) published usability research (Baymard, NN/g, academic meta-analyses, field experiments), (b) regulators' primary texts (EU Consumer Rights Directive, UK CMA guidance), (c) **live page captures made on 2026-10-10** of GetYourGuide, Viator, Airbnb Experiences, Klook and Headout (Prague listings), and (d) vendor/platform documentation. Vendor benchmark numbers are explicitly labelled as such. Items the research could not source are listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

---

## Key question 1 — What does published usability research say about card information density, image-first cards, "from" price anchoring, and showing "what's included" on cards vs. detail pages?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced card recipe is a small, *consistent* set of fields — thumbnail, title/type, permanently visible price, rating average + count, meaningful variations — plus 1–3 category-specific attributes, each visually distinct; Baymard finds 50–64 % of sites get this wrong, and hard-to-scan list info "hurt participants almost as much as having no list info at all." For pricing, the strongest evidence (StubHub field experiment, UK DBT and CMA work, partitioned-pricing meta-analysis) is that *mandatory* charges must be in the headline; a "from" price is only defensible when the add-ons are genuinely optional and the total is shown as soon as it can be computed. "What's included" is a detail-page element in all the research and all five marketplaces; cards carry at most 1–2 inclusion chips ("Skip the line", "Pickup available").

### Cited Findings

**Card / list-item anatomy (Baymard)**
- 50 % of e-commerce sites in Baymard's benchmark "failed to display adequate list item attributes"; the five essential attributes for every listing are (1) price — "must be permanently visible"; hiding it (e.g. "See price in cart") caused some users to drop products; (2) product title *and/or* type — keep titles to 3–4 lines on mobile, longer text reads as description; (3) thumbnail — listings without one "were often ignored and seen as 'incomplete'"; (4) user rating average **and** number of ratings; (5) meaningful variations (colour/size/material). Add roughly 1–3 category-specific attributes (e.g. screen size for laptops, age range for toys, water resistance for outdoor gear). Published May 30, 2023 (Iva Olah). — [Baymard, "What Information to Display in Product Listings (50% Get It Wrong)"](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-listing-information)
- 64 % of sites "failed to adequately display list item attributes"; 64 % fail to include the same attributes consistently across similar items and 40 % fail to make each attribute visually distinct. "Hard-to-scan list info hurt participants almost as much as having no list info at all." Fixes: state an attribute's unavailability explicitly instead of hiding it; present specs as bullets; in tight grid views a single line works "if elements are styled differently" (font variations + spacing); undifferentiated titles that mix name, type and features made users miss specific details. Published Aug 22, 2023. — [Baymard, "List Item Design"](https://baymard.com/research-articles/list-item-design-ecommerce)
- Guideline #452 "Price clarity in product lists and search results": without visible prices users can't scan list items efficiently to judge fit and may discard them (detail behind paywall). — [Baymard guideline #452](https://baymard.com/guidelines/452-price-clarity-in-product-lists-and-search-results)
- 86 % of sites don't show a "price per unit" for products sold in varying quantities; without it participants calculated manually ("I just divided [$42 by 4]"), "users aren't always correct in their calculations", cut comparisons short, or abandoned suitable items. Recommended format: total price dominant, per-unit price secondary but easy to find (e.g. Zooplus "€6.40 / kg", Walmart "77.7¢ ea"). Published Jul 18, 2023. — [Baymard, "Price Per Unit (86% Don't)"](https://baymard.com/blog/price-per-unit)

**Card / list-item anatomy (NN/g)**
- Minimum per listing: "Concise names that contain important and meaningful product characteristics"; "Photos large enough to identify a known item and show differences between items"; "Indication of other available colors, styles, or options"; price ("lack of prices is a huge usability problem on B2B sites"). Helpful extras: customer ratings; badges ("Icons or similar indications to mark items as popular, new, on sale, and so on"); availability; quick view; alternate images on hover. More information on listings is a growing trend but only "as long as the information is well-curated and uncluttered"; users expect "enough differentiating information" to decide whether a click is worth it, especially on mobile. Subcategory navigation can "reduce the number of items listed and avoid choice overload." Research base: 49 e-commerce sites. Published Aug 12, 2018 (Aurora Harley). — [NN/g, "UX Guidelines for Ecommerce Homepages, Category Pages, and Product Listing Pages"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-homepages-listing-pages/)

**Detail page anatomy (NN/g, Baymard)**
- NN/g product-page must-haves: descriptive product name; recognizable image(s) with enlarged view; "Price, including any additional product-specific charges"; clear options and a way to select them; availability; "Clear way to add an item to the cart, and clear feedback when it has been added"; "Concise and informative product description" — "Be complete, but not wordy or fluffy", "Don't waste the first few lines of product descriptions"; comparison tables rather than prose for data-heavy specs; explain unfamiliar terms. Showing free shipping on the product page changed how users judged the price (Casper vs Tuft & Needle example). Reviews: users want "summaries of the reviews" and to "quickly locate both favorable and negative reviews", with reviewer context (age, size, use case). Related-product recommendations are "nice to have", should be limited to highly relevant items; "showing too many suggestions on a product page can distract from or obscure important product information." 49 sites studied. Published Nov 24, 2019 (Katie Sherwin). — [NN/g, "UX Guidelines for Ecommerce Product Pages"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/)
- Baymard product-page benchmark (published Oct 24, 2023, updated Mar 18, 2026): only 48 % of desktop sites, 38 % of mobile sites and 36 % of apps have "decent" or better product-page UX; **67 % don't provide a total order cost estimate near the buy section** (recommendation: show the lowest full order cost by default plus a shipping/tax calculator); 81 % don't show price per unit; 44 % don't display or link the return policy from the main product content; 37 % lack "in scale" images while 42 % of users try to judge size from images. — [Baymard, "Product Page UX Best Practices 2026"](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- Baymard: 43 % of product pages don't show estimated shipping costs (article title statistic). — [Baymard, "Product Pages Need to Show 'Estimated Shipping Costs' (Yet 43% of Sites Don't)"](https://baymard.com/blog/show-shipping-costs-on-product-pages)

**"From" prices, drip pricing and partitioned pricing — field/academic evidence**
- StubHub field experiment (Blake, Moshary, Sweeney & Tadelis, *Marketing Science* 2021; experiment began fall 2015): showing fees up-front reduced both quantity and quality of purchases; "the effect of salience on quality accounts for at least 28% of the overall revenue decline"; click-stream data "show that price shrouding makes price comparisons difficult and results in consumers spending more than they would otherwise"; users shown fees late spent ≈21 % more and were ≈14 % more likely to complete a purchase (secondary summaries; verify against the paper). — [Working paper PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/stadelis/AIP.pdf); [Berkeley Haas newsroom, Feb 9, 2021](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/buyer-beware-massive-experiment-shows-why-ticket-sellers-hit-you-with-hidden-fees-drip-pricing/); [Deceptive.design summary](https://deceptive.design/articles/price-salience-and-product-choice)
- UK Department for Business and Trade research (published 4 Sep 2023): drip pricing present in 54 % of entertainment providers, 56 % hospitality, 72 % transport & communication; cost to UK online consumers put at £1.6 bn/yr (report range £595 m–£3.5 bn excluding delivery fees). — [GOV.UK, "Estimating the prevalence and impact of drip pricing"](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/estimating-the-prevalence-and-impact-of-drip-pricing)
- Partitioned-pricing meta-analysis (Abraham & Hamilton, 2018; 149 observations, 27 papers, N = 12,878): consumers prefer partitioned over all-inclusive pricing "when the total price is absent, when prices are higher, when surcharges are typical for the category, when surcharges seem beneficial, and when the product is utilitarian." (Search summary labelled the venue JCR; the DOI prefix 0022-2437 is *Journal of Marketing Research* — verify before citing the venue.) — [SAGE DOI 10.1177/0022243718800724](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022243718800724)
- UK CMA final price-transparency guidance (CMA209): mandatory charges belong in the headline price — "simply presenting a charge separately to the headline price or describing it as an extra service does not make it optional"; drip pricing (compulsory charges added later) and partitioned pricing (components without a total, unless the total cannot reasonably be calculated in advance) are prohibited; unavoidable local taxes/resort fees must be in the advertised travel price "even if … paid by the customer at the hotel or destination." Draft wording: a genuinely optional add-on need not be in the headline price, but traders "should include these charges in the total price if it is reasonably foreseeable that most consumers will need to pay them." — [GOV.UK CMA209](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209); [GOV.UK summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary); [Draft guidance PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b1fef0dcbf6343e9a28cc/Draft_price_transparency_guidance.pdf); law-firm digest: [Gordons LLP](https://www.gordonsllp.com/timeline/cma-publishes-final-guidance-on-price-transparency/)
- CMA consultation summary (18 Nov 2025): "expandable disclosures and linked pages with material pricing information are not reliable ways of complying … all relevant pricing information should be presented clearly, upfront, where the consumer is likely to see it." — [Summary of responses PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b20bd84a267da57d705f7/Summary_of_responses_18.11.25_1.pdf)

**Choice overload (relevant to how many options/attributes to show)**
- Chernev, Böckenholt & Goodman (2015, *J. Consumer Psychology*) meta-analysis: choice overload is reliably produced by decision-task difficulty, choice-set complexity, preference uncertainty, and an effort-minimising goal; when moderators are accounted for, assortment size has a significant overall effect (contradicting the ≈0 average of Scheibehenne et al. 2010). — [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual/)

**Where the marketplaces put "what's included" (live captures, 2026-10-10 — detail in Q3)**
- All five marketplaces keep inclusions on the detail page under a dedicated heading ("Includes" GYG; "What's Included" Viator; "What's included" inside Airbnb's "Listing highlights" and "Things to know"; "Inclusions/Exclusions" Headout; Klook behind "Package options"). Cards carry only chip-style hints: GYG "Skip the line • Small group", "Pickup available", "Optional audio guide"; Viator "Free Cancellation"; Headout "Free cancellation" badge. — [GYG Prague](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/); [Viator Prague](https://www.viator.com/Prague/d462-ttd); [Headout Prague](https://www.headout.com/things-to-do-city-prague/)

### Inferences
- A stag-activity card that shows image → badge → "location · duration" → title → 1–2 inclusion chips → rating (avg + count) → "From €X / person" (or "per group up to N") matches both the research minimum and what GYG/Viator/Airbnb converge on; anything more (full inclusions list, long description) belongs on the detail page per NN/g's "well-curated and uncluttered" rule.
- Because Baymard found 67 % of sites fail to show a total near the buy section and users mis-compute unit maths, a group-booking card/detail should compute "€X × N = €Y" rather than leave it to the organiser.
- The "from" prefix is legally and behaviourally safe only if nothing mandatory is hidden behind it; a group minimum that *will* apply to most buyers is closer to a mandatory charge in the CMA's "reasonably foreseeable" sense and should surface before the add-to-cart moment.

### Gaps
- No Baymard or NN/g guideline specifically on "from €X" *range* pricing for variant products was accessible (Baymard's relevant guidelines #792/#817 are paywalled).
- No published study measures image-first vs text-first card layouts for *experiences* specifically; evidence is from general e-commerce.
- Blake et al. headline percentages (21 %, 14 %) come from secondary summaries; the exact published figures were not verified against the Marketing Science text.

## Key question 2 — What is known about add-on/upsell UI performance (inline checkbox vs post-add modal vs cart cross-sell), opt-in vs pre-selected (legal/dark-pattern angle), number of options, increment vs new total, grouping?

### Takeaway
Independent research says relevance and non-intrusiveness matter more than placement: Baymard found 52 % of sites show irrelevant cart/added-to-cart cross-sells, "even 1 questionable recommendation" makes users ignore all of them, and interstitial cross-sells that force a decision are perceived as aggressive (66 % of users hitting Amazon's step showed extreme frustration). Pre-selected paid add-ons are illegal in the EU (CRD Art. 22) and the UK (CMA guidance, 18 Nov 2025: "You can't use pre-ticked boxes or other forms of automatic opt-in for optional extras"; forced "tick to avoid paying" is also barred), and regulators have forced airlines to switch to opt-in. Attach-rate numbers for checkbox vs modal vs post-purchase exist only as vendor claims (roughly 3–25 %, highly offer-dependent); no independent head-to-head test was found.

### Cited Findings

**Placement and intrusiveness (independent research)**
- Baymard (Jan 12, 2021): 52 % of benchmarked desktop sites present cart or "Added to Cart" cross-sells that are "either completely irrelevant or based only on what other customers bought"; users "are quick to dismiss" unrelated recommendations and "even 1 questionable recommendation" caused users to lose faith in all suggestions; irrelevant offers make the cart feel "pushy". Six tactics: dynamic (not fixed) number of suggestions; prioritise complementary items over alternatives at checkout; label the logic ("Frequently Bought Together"); prioritise same-theme/use-case groups (e.g. "Winter Essentials"); put compatibility-dependent/necessary items first; show promotions only at meaningful order levels. The "Added to Cart" overlay is treated as an acceptable cross-sell location when relevant (Argos, Crutchfield). — [Baymard, "Product Recommendations in the Cart"](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-recommendations-cart)
- Baymard checkout benchmark: injected cross-sells that "require users to make an active decision (e.g., to accept or reject an offer) to be able to proceed to the next checkout step are, by users, often perceived as highly aggressive"; in one test 66 % of users who hit a separate Amazon cross-sell step showed extreme frustration. — [Baymard, Cross-Sell step benchmark](https://baymard.com/checkout-usability/benchmark/step-type/cross-sell)
- Baymard guideline #578 (updated July 2026): unrelated suggestions reduce confidence in the site's cross-sells, including the relevant ones. — [Baymard guideline #578](https://baymard.com/guidelines/578-adapting-cross-sells-to-the-user-s-context)
- Baymard "Added to Cart confirmation": an overlay "can be a highly effective method of confirming the addition of an item, allowing users to review the just-added item and key order information. Overlays that lack adequate details risk users losing confidence"; ambiguous next-step labels made participants struggle to pick continue-shopping vs cart vs checkout. — [Baymard, Added to Cart Confirmation examples](https://www.baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/added-to-cart-confirmation)
- NN/g: add-to-cart feedback should be clear and persistent (overlay/layer or confirmation screen with "Continue Shopping"); related products "nice to have", keep to highly relevant, too many distract from product info. — [NN/g product pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/)
- Growth.design GoDaddy teardown: a pre-selected "Domain Protection" and a default 2-year term raised the advertised £1 to £18.96 at checkout ("why is it suddenly £18.96?"); the label "No Domain Protection" made the user feel unprotected; biases named: priming, salience, price anchoring, fear-based tactics, reactance; the redesign shows all options up front and avoids pre-selected paid add-ons. — [growth.design, "GoDaddy: How to improve checkout flows ethically"](https://growth.design/case-studies/godaddy-checkout-ux)

**Platform rules that encode good add-on UX (Shopify post-purchase offers)**
- Shopify post-purchase UX guidelines: accept button is primary with label pattern "Pay now • Product total price"; decline button placed under accept, less prominent but easy to see, fixed text "Decline upsell offer"; required money lines (subtotal, shipping, taxes, total; repeated after long descriptions); "Display a maximum of three consecutive upsell offers"; show discounted price next to original. — [Shopify.dev post-purchase UX guidelines](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/checkout/product-offers/post-purchase/ux-guidelines); [upsell page](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/checkout/product-offers/post-purchase/ux-guidelines/upsell)

**Attach-rate / conversion benchmarks — ALL VENDOR CLAIMS, not independent**
- Product-page (pre-purchase) upsells 8–15 % take rate; thank-you-page upsells 3–8 % (EA Upsell guide; GrowthSuite gives the same 3–8 % post-purchase range). — [EasyApps benchmarks](https://easyappsecom.com/guides/shopify-upsell-conversion-benchmarks); [GrowthSuite benchmarks](https://www.growthsuite.net/resources/shopify-upsell-cross-sell/complete-guide/benchmarks)
- Carthook (via Loopwork): post-purchase redirect-page upsells ≈4 % on average, optimised funnels ≈10 %. — [Loopwork](https://www.loopwork.co/blog/shopify-post-purchase-upsell-boost-aov)
- LaunchTip 2026 roundup: post-purchase 15–25 %; GrowthSuite beauty data 15–22 % for well-matched offers vs 3–7 % for poorly matched. — [LaunchTip](https://www.launchtip.com/upsell-statistics-in-e-commerce-key-statistics-and-data/)
- EasySell: one-click add-on checkbox attach rates "typically run 18–25 %". — [EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/shopify-one-click-add-on-upsell-checkbox-aov-2026)
- Bluestout CRO case: a gift checkbox placed beside the add-to-cart button *slowed sales*; moving the option to the cart page and raising the button produced +6 % conversions (single-store anecdote). — [Bluestout](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/one-checkbox-that-slowed-sales-and-what-fixed-it/)
- Personizely's "post-add cross-sell slide-in" is a test *hypothesis* projecting 8–18 % attach-rate lift, not a result. — [Personizely](https://help.personizely.net/en/article/inspiration-test-whether-a-postadd-crosssell-slidein-increases-attach-rate-and-aov-r2srf3/)
- Tour-operator software vendor Singenuity: attach rate = bookings containing an add-on ÷ total bookings, track per add-on; a gap between counter and online attach (example "40 % in person but 8 % online") "points to booking-flow placement rather than weak guest interest"; photo add-ons "$5 to $9 in added revenue per guest" (no methodology). — [Singenuity glossary](https://singenuity.com/glossary/add-on-attach-rate)
- Expedia Group 2025 Traveler Value Index (Wakefield Research, 11,000 respondents, 11 markets; published June 10, 2025): "Complimentary add-ons are also shown to be the most appealing promotion when booking airfare, car rentals, and activity bookings"; UK/AU snapshots: complimentary add-on 48 %, package booking discount 42 %, early booking discount 41 %. — [Expedia Group TVI highlights](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/2025-traveler-value-index-highlights); [UK snapshot PDF](https://partner.expediagroup.com/content/dam/unified/partner/documents/one-pagers/expedia-group-tvi-snapshot-air-car-activity_en-gb.pdf)

**Default state — law and regulators**
- EU Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Art. 22: "Before the consumer is bound by the contract or offer, the trader shall seek the express consent of the consumer to any extra payment in addition to the remuneration agreed upon for the trader's main contractual obligation. If the trader has not obtained the consumer's express consent but has inferred it by using default options … the consumer shall be entitled to reimbursement of this payment." Art. 8(2): order button must read "order with obligation to pay" or an unambiguous equivalent. — [EUR-Lex CELEX:32011L0083](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011L0083); Commission summary: "Pre-ticked boxes on an order form may not be used for such payments." — [European Commission, CRD page](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/consumer-contract-law/consumer-rights-directive_en?etrans=da)
- UK CMA, "Getting consent for additional charges when selling online" (18 Nov 2025): "You can't use pre-ticked boxes or other forms of automatic opt-in for optional extras"; consent cannot come from "not changing a default option, for example by not removing a tick from a pre-ticked box" nor from "opting out of an extra, for example if they are required to tick a box to avoid paying"; businesses must "clearly explain any additional payments that consumers can choose to make" and "give customers a way to check and confirm what they are paying for". Allowed examples: unticked gift-wrap box the customer "must actively tick"; a round-up donation where the customer "must select either 'Round up' or 'No, thanks'". Not allowed: express delivery pre-chosen; insurance auto-added to basket ("adding something automatically to a basket is not express consent"); a sample where "the default is that the customer has to pay". Unconsented charges need not be paid and are refundable; fines up to 10 % of turnover or £300,000. — [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/getting-consent-for-additional-charges-when-selling-online/getting-consent-for-additional-charges-when-selling-online)
- CMA enforcement drive (Nov 2025): investigations into eight businesses over fees, misleading time-limited offers "and/or the practice of automatically opting consumers in for optional charges"; advisory letters to 100 businesses; concerns found in 14 sectors. — [GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-launches-major-consumer-protection-drive-focused-on-online-pricing-practices)
- Airline precedents: Air New Zealand changed its opt-out travel insurance to opt-in after NZ Commerce Commission action (2015). — [Runway Girl Network](https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2015/04/regulators-hit-back-at-air-new-zealands-opt-out-add-ons/); Jetstar agreed to drop pre-selected "opt-out" services (2016). — [InDaily](https://www.indailysa.com.au/news/archive/2016/03/17/jetstar-takes-ticks-off-after-ticking-off); AirAsia warned and moved checked baggage to "opt in" (NZ ComCom 2016). — [Commerce Commission NZ](https://comcom.govt.nz/news-and-media/media-releases/2016/airasia-warned-over-opt-out-pricing-and-processing-fee); Virgin Australia and Jetstar stopped pre-selecting travel insurance after ACCC pressure. — [ITIJ](https://www.itij.com/latest/news/virgin-australia-stops-pre-selection-travel-insurance-its-booking-platform)
- "Must-opt" (forced choice before continuing) as an airline response to the opt-in rule: Barry, Hogan & Torres (ISD 2016) — LCCs "have responded to the legal requirement that all optional extras should only be presented [on an opt-in basis] by using 'grey' Web design patterns such as the 'must-opt'"; studied via verbal protocols on two LCCs; the Galway version argues must-opt "appears to contravene" EU law (UCPD Arts 5–7, Reg. 1008/2008 Art. 23(1)). — [AISeL](https://aisel.aisnet.org/isd2014/proceedings2016/General/3); [Univ. of Galway record](https://researchrepository.universityofgalway.ie/entities/publication/82833dd7-87d8-4ded-a1f2-9a5a5f015cf3)
- Mathur et al., "Dark Patterns at Scale" (ACM CSCW 2019): ~53K product pages on ~11K shopping sites, 1,818 dark-pattern instances in 15 types; "Sneak into Basket" (adding products without consent) found on 7 sites. — [Princeton project page](https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns); [arXiv 1907.07032](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.07032v2)
- European Commission behavioural study (May 2022): 97 % of popular websites used by EU citizens contained at least one dark pattern; "preselection of options" listed among them. — [UOC news summary](https://www.uoc.edu/en/news/2022/217-unfair-online-commercial-practices)
- EU Digital Fairness Act: not yet proposed; planned as a Q4 2026 initiative, scope contested; consultation coverage names "pre-checked boxes, or hidden opt-outs" as targets. — [Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2025/08/01/eu-consults-on-digital-fairness-act-big-changes-ahead-for-consumer-facing-platforms/); [PinMeTo status note](https://www.pinmeto.com/news/eu-digital-fairness-act-scope-split-2026/)
- Airline ancillary scale (IdeaWorks 2025 Yearbook, released Sep 9, 2025; 61 airlines, FY2024): ancillary revenue per passenger +2.5 % globally while fare revenue per passenger fell 3.8 %; Norse Atlantic first to exceed $100/passenger for a full year. — [IdeaWorks press release PDF](https://ideaworkscompany.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Press-Release-195-Ancillary-Yearbook-1.pdf)

**Increment vs new total; number of options; grouping**
- Baymard: 67 % of sites fail to show a total order cost near the buy section; recommended: lowest full order cost by default. — [Baymard product page 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- CMA: partitioned pricing prohibited unless the total cannot reasonably be calculated. — [GOV.UK CMA209 summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary)
- Shopify: offer total shown in the accept button ("Pay now • total") plus full money breakdown. — [Shopify.dev](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/checkout/product-offers/post-purchase/ux-guidelines)
- Baymard: avoid a fixed count of suggestions (default of "5 suggestions" criticised); group by theme and label the group. — [Baymard cart recommendations](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-recommendations-cart)
- Chernev et al. 2015: larger sets overload when options are complex and preferences uncertain. — [Kellogg](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2015/when-product-assortment-leads-to-choice-overload-a-conceptual/)
- Live qualitative signal: GetYourGuide's AI review summaries on two Prague products flag unclear optional costs — shooting range: "Some mention clearer details on packages, included gun counts, and extra costs would help"; beer spa: "clearer details on what is included, especially whether massages are optional and their cost, would help" (and "Includes … 20-minute massage (only if option chosen)"). — [GYG shooting range](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-shooting-range-experience-with-up-to-10-guns-t188718/); [GYG beer spa](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-beer-bath-with-unlimited-beer-t230002/)

### Inferences
- The evidence favours **opt-in, inline add-ons on the detail page or in the added-to-cart overlay**, each with its own price and an updated total, over a forced interstitial; a post-add overlay is acceptable only if it shows the just-added item and key order info and is dismissible without a decision.
- Legally, the safe pattern in EU/UK is: unticked checkbox (or explicit "Add / No thanks" pair), per-add-on price visible before the tick, and no "tick to avoid paying" construction; a group minimum or mandatory service fee must be in the headline/total, not an add-on.
- Keep add-on sets small and themed (e.g. "Make it a private show", "Add transport") and show the *new group total* next to the increment, since Baymard's unit-price study shows users mis-compute and abandon when they must do the arithmetic.

### Gaps
- No independent A/B study comparing inline checkbox vs post-add modal vs cart cross-sell on attach rate was found; all numeric benchmarks are vendor-published.
- No published attach-rate data from tour-booking platforms (FareHarbor, Peek, Rezdy, Bokun) was found.
- The full text of Barry/Hogan/Torres (sample size, results) was behind a 403; only the abstract was accessible.
- No source quantifies the effect of per-person vs flat add-on pricing on attach rate.

## Key question 3 — How do GetYourGuide, Viator, Airbnb Experiences, Klook and Headout structure activity cards and detail pages in 2025–2026? (Live captures, 2026-10-10, Prague)

### Takeaway
All five converge on an image-first card with ≤7 text elements — badge, location·duration/category, title, 1–2 feature chips, rating + review count, "From €X" (with strike-through when discounted) — and a detail page ordered: title + rating + provider → trust/flexibility chips (free cancellation, reserve-now-pay-later, duration, language, group type) → short abstract → Highlights (bullets) → Full description → Includes / Not included → Meeting point → Know-before-you-go → sticky booking widget ("From €X per person / per group up to N", participants, date) → reviews with AI summary. Optional extras are sold as *options/packages* chosen in the booking widget (GYG "Select participants and date" → options; Klook "Package options" + "Select options"; Headout "Optional upgrades"), not as post-add modals; Airbnb Experiences has no add-ons at all.

## Key question 4 — For group organisers paying for many people (stag/bachelor, team events), which pricing displays reduce confusion?

### Takeaway
Marketplaces disambiguate with explicit unit labels — "per person" (GYG/Viator), "per guest" (Airbnb), "per group up to N" (GYG), "Per group" / "Price varies by group size" (Viator) — and show the computed total only after party size is chosen. Baymard's unit-price research (86 % of sites missing it; users mis-compute and abandon) and its finding that 67 % of sites fail to show a total near the buy section argue for always showing "€X × N = €Y" plus any group-minimum rule before add-to-cart; the CMA treats a foreseeable mandatory charge as part of the total.

### Cited Findings
- GetYourGuide labels per-group products on the card and widget as "From €137 per group up to 2" with a "Participants x 1" stepper; per-person products as "From €99 per person / Adult x 1". — [GYG beer spa](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-beer-bath-with-unlimited-beer-t230002/); [GYG shooting range](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-shooting-range-experience-with-up-to-10-guns-t188718/)
- Viator shows "from €79.00 per person" in the widget, and on cards/promoted carousels either "Per group" or "Price varies by group size"; defaults the party to "2 Adults"; private big-group tour copy: "from around US$2,400 for a group of up to 15 people". — [Viator beer spa](https://www.viator.com/tours/Prague/Beer-Spa-and-Salt-Cave-for-1-12-people-single-bath/d462-402036P1); [Viator Prague](https://www.viator.com/Prague/d462-ttd)
- Airbnb Experiences prices "From €56, per guest"; group size appears only under "Things to know" (up to 11 guests). — [Airbnb experience 710334](https://www.airbnb.com/experiences/710334)
- Baymard: without a unit price users divided totals by hand, "aren't always correct in their calculations", assumed units, cut comparisons short, or abandoned; show total dominant + per-unit secondary. — [Baymard price per unit](https://baymard.com/blog/price-per-unit)
- Baymard: 67 % of sites don't provide a total order cost estimate near the buy section. — [Baymard product page 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)
- NN/g: price must include "any additional product-specific charges". — [NN/g product pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/)
- CMA: charges that are unavoidable, or that "it is reasonably foreseeable that most consumers will need to pay", belong in the total; partitioned pricing without a total is prohibited unless the total can't be calculated. — [CMA209 summary](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209/providing-clear-and-accurate-information-about-prices-summary); [Draft guidance PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b1fef0dcbf6343e9a28cc/Draft_price_transparency_guidance.pdf)
- Booking-software conventions (vendor docs): TourCMS defaults to "Price per person" with optional whole-group rates and group-size tiers where "the booking engine shows the price per person for the selected party size". — [TourCMS rate-type pricing](https://support.tourcms.com/hc/en-us/articles/9249936860177-Rate-type-pricing-article); Captain Book: single flat fee for private tours/venue rentals, no tiers. — [Captain Book pricing](https://knowledge.captainbook.io/en/articles/13714971-configuring-pricing); Konfetti: flat base-group rate plus surcharge per extra person, recommended "for team events, bachelor parties, and venue rentals". — [Konfetti help](https://partner.gokonfetti.com/en/how-can-i-set-my-prices-capacities)
- Group-floor confusion is visible in live reviews: GYG AI summaries ask for "clearer details on packages, included gun counts, and extra costs" and "whether massages are optional and their cost". — [GYG shooting range](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-shooting-range-experience-with-up-to-10-guns-t188718/)

### Inferences
- A line like "€45 / person × 12 = €540" with a secondary note "Group minimum €400 applies" (or, when the floor binds, "€400 group minimum · €33.33 / person") follows Baymard's total-dominant/unit-secondary format and the CMA's total-price rule.
- Label every price unit explicitly ("per person", "per group up to N"); never mix unlabelled per-person and per-group prices in one list (Viator's "Price varies by group size" is the fallback when the unit can't be fixed on a card).
- For organiser-pays flows, the detail page should compute the group total as soon as a party size is known (Viator pre-fills 2 adults; a stag flow can pre-fill the saved group size).

### Gaps
- No usability study specifically on group-organiser booking (one payer, many travellers) was found; the per-person × N recommendation is extrapolated from unit-price and total-cost research.
- No evidence on whether showing a per-person equivalent of a flat group price increases or decreases conversion.

## Key question 5 — How do chat/AI assistants in commerce present and confirm add-ons ("add a private show to the boat party"), confirm price impact, and allow undo?

### Takeaway
Across voice-commerce guidelines (Amazon Alexa, Google), agentic-checkout products (OpenAI/ACP, Google Pay agentic checkout) and classic HCI guidance (NN/g), the consistent pattern is: implicit confirmation for parameters, **explicit confirmation before any action that is hard to undo or costs money**, the confirmation restating the specific item, the new total "including tax and fees" and the payment method, outcome-labelled buttons, and a price-match guard (purchase proceeds only if the final price ≤ the confirmed total). Undo after the fact is handled by a reversible cart state or a receipt-style summary with an undo control; UK CMA guidance (9 Mar 2026) makes the business liable for what its agent does and requires it to "properly obtain any necessary consents."

### Cited Findings

**HCI guidance on confirmation vs undo**
- NN/g (Jakob Nielsen, Feb 18, 2018; reviewed Aug 7, 2026): confirm only serious actions, especially those that "cannot be undone"; undo is preferable where possible; "if you cry wolf too many times, people will stop paying attention"; dialogs should name the item and effect rather than ask "Are you sure?"; label buttons with outcomes ("Delete file" / "Keep file"), and do not pre-select the confirming option. — [NN/g, Confirmation Dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)
- NN/g heuristic #3 User Control and Freedom: support undo/redo and a clearly marked emergency exit. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-control-and-freedom/)
- Google conversation-design guidelines: explicit confirmation of actions is "rare" — "Double-check with the user prior to performing an action that would be difficult to undo, for example, deleting user data, completing a transaction"; implicit confirmation of parameters "most of the time"; explicit parameter confirmation only "when the cost of misunderstanding the user is high, for example, names, addresses, texts to be shared". — [Google Assistant conversation design: Confirmations](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/confirmations?hl=en)
- Amazon Alexa Haus (handling payments): "The customer will need to give explicit confirmation (a 'yes' answer) before you process the payment"; the confirmation "needs to include the full details of their purchase to assure them of the final total" plus tax/fees, "the payment method charged, and the delivery address if applicable"; example: "Your total $6.85, including tax, charged to your card ending in 5-5-5-5. Should I place your order now?"; customers can say "no" to any call to action and can ask to remove items before checkout. — [Alexa Haus](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/alexa/alexa-haus/handling-payments)
- Alexa Shopping Kit: Alexa reads product title, price and delivery estimate, then asks whether to buy; "Yes" triggers "Order placed"; voice-PIN/voice-profile authorization checks. — [Alexa Shopping Kit docs](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/docs/alexa/alexa-shopping/about-alexa-shopping-actions-for-alexa-skills.html)

**Agentic commerce products (2025–2026)**
- OpenAI "Buy it in ChatGPT" (Sept 2025): users "can tap 'Buy,' confirm their order, shipping, and payment details, and complete the purchase without ever leaving the chat"; merchant remains merchant of record and "accepts or declines the order". — [OpenAI announcement](https://openai.com/index/buy-it-in-chatgpt/) (page returned 403 to the fetcher; wording from search index); developer docs: ChatGPT "renders a checkout experience embedded in ChatGPT's UI", "sets a maximum chargeable amount and expiry based on what the user has selected", merchant "accepts or declines the order"; "OpenAI is not the merchant of record". — [OpenAI commerce key concepts](https://developers.openai.com/commerce/guides/key-concepts). Reports say OpenAI narrowed Instant Checkout's scope in March 2026 toward discovery with merchant-run checkout. — [eesel summary](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-instant-checkout-2026)
- Google Pay agentic checkout (US/AU, select merchants): purchase proceeds only if the final price is the same as or lower than "the total price you confirmed"; if the price rises "the purchase doesn't complete and you aren't charged"; order updates by email/notification; cancellations go through the merchant. — [Google Pay help](https://support.google.com/googlepay/answer/16421147); flow: "Users confirm the purchase details and tap 'buy for me'". — [Lifehacker JP](https://www.lifehacker.jp/article/2511-google-is-rolling-out-agentic-checkout/)
- Agent payment protocols bind consent to merchant, amount and session via scoped tokens/signed mandates (secondary). — [PXP glossary](https://www.pxp.io/payments-glossary/agentic-commerce)

**Travel/airline chat assistants that modify a booking**
- FlySafair "Chat 2 Pay" on WhatsApp (1 Feb 2023, with Clickatell): customers "can add their checked luggage to an existing booking and make payment for that service" inside the chat — flow: send "Hi", choose "My Booking", pay via a secure payment link; article does not describe a price summary or confirmation message. — [Travel News](https://www.travelnews.co.za/node/60645)

- Expedia Romie (2024): builds/updates itineraries from saved items and group chats; no in-chat add-on confirmation described. — [Expedia Group IR](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2024/Put-Your-Trip-on-Autopilot-Expedia-Group-Introduces-New-Innovations-at-EXPLORE-to-Take-the-Stress-out-of-Travel-and-Enhance-Partner-Experience/default.aspx)

**Regulatory frame for AI-assisted selling**

### Inferences
- For "add a private show to the boat party": the assistant should (1) resolve the target unambiguously (ask "which day/package?" if ambiguous — Rep AI/Google parameter rule), (2) reply with a receipt-style card naming the add-on, its unit price, the quantity it applies to (×N people or flat), and the **new group total**, (3) require an explicit "Add it" (outcome-labelled, not pre-selected) only when money is committed, and (4) offer a one-tap "Undo" that reverts the whole batch — matching Alexa Haus, Google's explicit-confirmation rule, NN/g's outcome-labelled buttons, and the Amazon batch-undo pattern.
- Because the price can change between quote and commit, mirror Google Pay's guard: apply the change only if the recomputed total equals or is below the confirmed figure, otherwise re-confirm.
- Cart edits (non-payment) can use implicit confirmation + undo; payment commits need explicit confirmation — the same split the planner's `ApplyEditsNode` already embodies for ADD/REMOVE/REPLACE edits.

### Gaps
- No public documentation from Expedia, Booking.com, Klarna or Shopify (Sidekick/Shop) describes an in-chat add-on confirmation or undo flow; only launch-level coverage exists.
- No NN/g article specifically on agentic shopping confirmation was found.
- Airline WhatsApp add-bag flows (FlySafair, Spirit) are described without the confirmation/summary step; whether a price is read back before payment is unverified.
- The ACP checkout spec's own text on buyer confirmation was not retrieved (only the key-concepts page).
