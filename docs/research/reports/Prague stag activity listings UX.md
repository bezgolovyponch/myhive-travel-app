# Price the extras your rivals hide

Trivlu's cheapest competitive win is transparency: **five of the six UK stag aggregators that sell Prague hide every activity price, and not one competitor on any continent prints the price of an add-on** — extras are listed by name and closed by phone or quote form. The only sellers that show prices (Funktion Events in GBP, Prague Stag Fun in EUR, and the GetYourGuide/Viator-class marketplaces) are also the only ones that label the unit, and the published evidence from Baymard, NN/g, a StubHub field experiment and the UK CMA's 2025 price-transparency guidance all point the same way: a permanently visible price with an explicit "per person / per group" label, a lean card of five to seven elements, inclusions kept on the detail page, and optional extras sold as **unticked opt-in options with their own price and the new group total** — pre-selected or forced-choice extras are unlawful in the EU and UK. For chat-assisted booking the proven pattern (Alexa, Google Pay agentic checkout, NN/g) is implicit confirmation for cart edits, an explicit outcome-labelled confirmation whenever money is committed, a receipt restating the new total, and a one-tap undo. The must-have Prague catalogue is roughly twenty recurring products — shooting, tank, karting, beer bike, party boat, bar crawl, brewery and beer spa, steak-and-show dinner, stag arrest, strip-club entry, performer-to-venue, limo/party-bus transfers, VIP tables — with bar crawls at €18–45, boats €40–70, shooting mostly €80–130 and steak-and-show €59–£68.50 per person, while standalone shows run **€159–649 per group**. Adult extras are sold everywhere as a 15-minute show with a private room, a guide, a bar minimum and an 18+ line; an honest-but-neutral register ("private show by a professional performer") beats both StagWeb's euphemism and Pissup's crudeness. For images, copying competitor photos is infringement in the UK, EU and Czechia with no commercial exception, hotlinking is fragile and barred by stock-site terms, so the set should come from Unsplash/Pexels/Pixabay/CC0 Commons plus supplier-licensed shots, cut to one 4:3 1200×900 frame with one colour grade, people visibly doing the activity, and prop-and-venue framing (curtain, feathers, champagne, neon) for adult items — which also keeps the set usable in Google Ads in Germany.

## Five of six UK aggregators hide prices; the transparent sellers label the unit

([StagWeb Target Shooting](https://www.stagweb.co.uk/stag-do/prague/activities/targetshooting)). ## Add-ons are named, never priced, and closed by a human

Across the UK, Prague and US sets, **no public page shows an add-on price, a per-person-versus-per-group label on an add-on, or a real checkbox**. Four presentation patterns exist. LNOF uses a bare bulleted list under "Add upgrades:" ("Lesbian Show, Male Handcuffed Dwarf, Roly Poly Stripper" on the steak dinner; "Dominatrix Upgrade, Extra Beer, Female Stripper" on the strip boat), with no price and no control ([LNOF Steak & Strip](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/activities/prague-stag-steak-%26-strip-dinner/); [LNOF Strip Boat](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/activities/prague-strip-boat/)). Chillisauce's "Add Ons" block shows a "Selected" label and the caveat "The per person price may change if the size of your group does" — the closest any site gets to an in-page selector ([Chillisauce Bar Crawl 10 Beers](https://www.chillisauce.com/stag/in-prague/do-bar-crawls/go-03d921d57fb47cdd6279)). Maximise has a "Choose option(s)" + "Add" control that is often empty, and Pissup lists nine extras under "Add-Ons Your Stag Might Enjoy" (from "Extra female stripper" to "Unlimited shots for 90 minutes") with selection deferred to "Add To Stag & See Prices" ([Maximise Top Night Out](https://stag.maximise.co.uk/prague-stag-do/top-night-out-activities); [Pissup Steak & Tits](https://www.pissup.com/prague-stag-do/activities/steak-tits/)). StagWeb and Funktion have no add-on section at all; options are bullets inside the inclusions with "Ask our team for more details" or "enquire" ([StagWeb Cheeky Boat Cruise](https://www.stagweb.co.uk/stag-do/prague/activities/cheeky-boat-cruise); [Funktion River Cruise + Stripper](https://www.funktionevents.co.uk/stag-do/czech-republic/prague/private-river-cruise-stripper-drinks)). Prague Stag Fun comes closest to pricing an extra, but only for consumables: its steak-and-show page has a "Hot add-ons" list of cross-links to separate adult products with no price, and a "Meal upgrades" list where finger food is "an extra 19 EUR / 459 CZK p.p." and an hour of unlimited beer "29 EUR / 725 CZK p.p." ([PSF Steak & Strip](https://www.praguestagfun.com/product/prague-premium-steak-and-strip-dinner/)). In the US the only priced extra found anywhere was Bachelor Vegas's flat "alcohol add-on package is $100 for a $100 in booze" ([Bachelor Vegas stripper party bus](https://www.bachelorvegas.com/nightlife/stripper-party-bus.html)).

Two data models coexist, often on the same site. In the first the add-on is a child of the activity (LNOF, Chillisauce bar crawls, Pissup, Maximise); in the second the upgraded product is a separate SKU — Chillisauce's cruise is one base product × {performer type} × {drinks} as five "Similar Options", Pissup offers nine steak-and-show menus as "Other options", StagWeb models shooting upgrades as six named tier cards, and Funktion sells every upgrade as its own priced line ([Chillisauce Strip Cruise](https://chillisauce.com/stag/in-prague/do-stripper-boat-parties/go-9a33fd325b151ae44229); [Pissup Steak & Tits](https://www.pissup.com/prague-stag-do/activities/steak-tits/); [StagWeb Target Shooting](https://www.stagweb.co.uk/stag-do/prague/activities/targetshooting)). Funktion's variant pricing is therefore the only public source of add-on deltas: Private River Cruise with Unlimited Drinks £68.50 versus with Stripper £80.00 (**about +£11.50**), Traditional Czech Dinner £47.00 versus with Stripper £61.00 (+£14), Hummer Limo Airport Transfer £525.00 versus with Stripper £550.00 (+£25, evidently per vehicle), Beer Boat £702.50 versus with Pizza £942.50 (+£240 per boat), and Steak & Strip £68.50 versus Steak & Lesbian Show £71.00 versus Steak & Roly Poly £63.50 ([Funktion Prague](https://www.funktionevents.co.uk/stag-do/czech-republic/prague)). These are arithmetic on listed prices, not statements by Funktion, and their unlabelled basis is itself the lesson.

The unit follows the cost driver everywhere it is visible. Prague Stag Fun prices anything that scales with headcount per person (dinners, transfers, shooting, cruises) and fixed-cost shows per group — "Prague Sexy Strip Show 159€ / group" with the line **"Price is per group - you share the cost!"**, XXXL show 224€/group, mud wrestling 499€/group, dwarf prank 306€/group ([PSF Sexy Strip Show](https://www.praguestagfun.com/product/prague-sexy-strip-show/); [PSF shop](https://www.praguestagfun.com/shop/)). Boats are per trip with a cap and a per-head overflow ("€567 per trip", at least 10 participants; "€500 for 11 people and €40 for each additional person"), and a boat-side strip show is a flat "€100" for 15 minutes ([alle.travel private boat party](https://alle.travel/en/prague/activities/45304-vltava-river-private-boat-party-with-drinks); [Expedia beer boat](https://www.expedia.com/things-to-do/prague-private-beer-boat-cruise-with-unlimited-beer.a48154985.activity-details); [alle.travel beer boat](https://alle.travel/en/prague/activities/45173-private-prague-beer-boat-cruise)). Shooting ranges push optional guns to pay-on-site ("Desert Eagle, 1 shot: 20 €", "always paid directly at the shooting range") ([Prague Ranger prices](https://www.pragueranger.cz/prices/)). Adult entertainment is sold both as standalone products and as a variant of mainstream ones (dinner + show, cruise + show, transfer + show, bar crawl + strip-club entry, spa + show, arrest + show), and the wording sits on a clear spectrum. StagWeb never uses "strip" or "stripper" in a product name — "Female Performer", "Dinner & Female Performer", "Cheeky Boat Cruise", "Gentlemens Club" — and its Dinner & Female Performer page never says what the show involves beyond "Private show" and "one last look at what he'll be missing" ([StagWeb Dinner & Female Performer](https://www.stagweb.co.uk/activities/dinner-female-performer); [StagWeb Prague activities](https://www.stagweb.co.uk/stag-do/prague/activities/)). LNOF and Funktion are explicit but formulaic ("Hot striptease show by one of our gorgeous strippers", "Fully nude show", "Sexy striptease show (15 minutes)"), and Pissup is crude ("Steak & Tits", "huge tits wherever you look") yet has the most explicit house rules: "Under no circumstances should you touch any of the women", "You'll be searched for weapons before you enter the club" ([LNOF Strip Boat](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/activities/prague-strip-boat/); [Funktion Stripper](https://www.funktionevents.co.uk/stag-do/czech-republic/prague/stripper); [Pissup Gold Show](https://www.pissup.com/prague-stag-do/activities/gold-show-package-prague/)). What is universal is the unit — **a 15-minute show** — and the reassurance set: private room, English-speaking guide, "surprise for the stag", a bar minimum spend ("If in a bar, minimum consumption 80kc … must be spent by each person"), and on Funktion alone an "18+ age restriction" bullet ([Funktion Stripper](https://www.funktionevents.co.uk/stag-do/czech-republic/prague/stripper)). The "tasteful" register that still sells relies on four moves: a neutral noun (performer, show, surprise guest), the 15-minute duration as the only concrete fact, framing the show as a groom prank rather than a service to the group, and pushing detail to "ask your specialist". Where strip-club entry is bundled into a bar crawl it is phrased as optional on the night ("Entry to a strip club if desired"), a way to sell one product to groups with and without the appetite ([LNOF Bar Crawl](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/activities/prague-guided-bar-crawl-nightclub-%26-strip-club-entry/)). Disclaimers are thin everywhere — LNOF, Funktion's listing and Prague Stag Fun's product pages carry no age, consent or conduct text at all — so stating 18+, performer professionalism (Prague Stag Fun's "professional Czech dancer and a bodyguard"), tipping and the bar minimum up front is a cheap trust differentiator ([LNOF Prague activities](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/stag-activities/prague/); [PSF Sexy Strip Show](https://www.praguestagfun.com/product/prague-sexy-strip-show/)).

## Research and regulators converge on visible unit prices and opt-in extras

The usability literature is unambiguous that the quote-wall is a defect, not a strategy. Baymard's listing benchmark names five attributes every list item needs — a price that "must be permanently visible" (hiding it behind "See price in cart" caused users to drop products), a title or type kept to three or four lines on mobile, a thumbnail (listings without one "were often ignored and seen as 'incomplete'"), the rating average **and** count, and meaningful variations — plus one to three category-specific attributes; **50 % of sites fail this, 64 % fail to show attributes consistently across similar items**, and "hard-to-scan list info hurt participants almost as much as having no list info at all" ([Baymard, product listing information](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-listing-information); [Baymard, list item design](https://baymard.com/research-articles/list-item-design-ecommerce)). NN/g's minimum is the same — concise names with meaningful characteristics, photos large enough to tell items apart, an indication of options, a price — with the warning that extra listing information helps only "as long as the information is well-curated and uncluttered" ([NN/g, listing pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-homepages-listing-pages/)). Group buyers suffer a specific arithmetic failure: **86 % of sites omit a per-unit price**, and without it users divided totals by hand, "aren't always correct in their calculations", cut comparisons short or abandoned; the fix is total price dominant and per-unit secondary ([Baymard, price per unit](https://baymard.com/blog/price-per-unit)). On the detail page **67 % of sites fail to show a total order cost near the buy section** ([Baymard, product page UX 2026](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-ecommerce-product-page-ux)), and NN/g requires the price to include "any additional product-specific charges" ([NN/g, product pages](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-product-pages/)).

"From" prices are only defensible when nothing mandatory hides behind them. The StubHub field experiment found that price shrouding "makes price comparisons difficult and results in consumers spending more than they would otherwise", with the salience effect on purchase quality accounting for at least 28 % of the revenue decline when fees were shown up front — evidence that drip pricing works for the seller precisely because it harms the buyer ([Blake et al. working paper](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/stadelis/AIP.pdf); [Berkeley Haas](https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/buyer-beware-massive-experiment-shows-why-ticket-sellers-hit-you-with-hidden-fees-drip-pricing/)). UK government research puts drip pricing in 54 % of entertainment providers and its cost to consumers at £1.6 bn a year ([GOV.UK, drip pricing](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/estimating-the-prevalence-and-impact-of-drip-pricing)), and the CMA's final guidance CMA209 now requires mandatory charges in the headline, prohibits partitioned pricing without a total, and says a charge "it is reasonably foreseeable that most consumers will need to pay" belongs in the total, adding that "expandable disclosures and linked pages … are not reliable ways of complying" ([CMA209](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209); [CMA summary of responses](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/691b20bd84a267da57d705f7/Summary_of_responses_18.11.25_1.pdf)). A group minimum that binds for most buyers is a foreseeable charge in that sense, which is why the floor should appear on the card or before add-to-cart rather than in checkout.

The five experience marketplaces captured live on 2026-10-10 have already converged on the research recipe. GetYourGuide's card reads badge → "PRAGUE · 2 HOURS" → title → one or two feature chips ("Skip the line • Small group", "Pickup available") → "4.9 (2,269)" → "From €99", and group-priced products say **"From €137 per group up to 2"** ([GYG Prague](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/); [GYG beer spa](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-beer-bath-with-unlimited-beer-t230002/)); Viator adds "Free Cancellation" and falls back to "Price varies by group size" when the unit cannot be fixed, and Airbnb prints "From €56, per guest" ([Viator Prague](https://www.viator.com/Prague/d462-ttd); [Airbnb Prague experiences](https://www.airbnb.com/s/Prague/experiences)). Inclusions are never on cards: all five keep them on the detail page under "Includes"/"What's Included"/"Inclusions", in a page order of title + rating + provider → trust chips → abstract → Highlights → full description → Includes/Not included → Meeting point → Know before you go → sticky widget → reviews ([GYG shooting range](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-shooting-range-experience-with-up-to-10-guns-t188718/); [Viator beer spa](https://www.viator.com/tours/Prague/Beer-Spa-and-Salt-Cave-for-1-12-people-single-bath/d462-402036P1)). Extras are sold as **options chosen inside the booking widget** — GYG's options step, Klook's "Package options", Headout's "Optional upgrades for a seamless experience" — not as post-add modals, and Airbnb Experiences has no add-ons at all ([Klook pub crawl](https://www.klook.com/en-GB/activity/191877-pub-crawl-with-drinks-karlovy-lazne-vip-entry-in-prague/); [Headout Prague Castle](https://www.headout.com/prague-castle-tickets/prague-castle-tickets-e-8905/); [Airbnb experience 710334](https://www.airbnb.com/experiences/710334)). The cost of unclear optional charges is visible in GYG's own AI review summaries for two Prague products: the shooting range's travellers want "clearer details on packages, included gun counts, and extra costs", and beer-spa guests want to know "whether massages are optional and their cost" ([GYG shooting range](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-shooting-range-experience-with-up-to-10-guns-t188718/); [GYG beer spa](https://www.getyourguide.com/prague-l10/prague-beer-bath-with-unlimited-beer-t230002/)).

On add-on mechanics the independent evidence says relevance and non-intrusiveness matter more than placement, and the law fixes the default state. Baymard found **52 % of sites show irrelevant cart cross-sells**, that "even 1 questionable recommendation" makes users ignore all of them, and that interstitials forcing an accept/reject decision before the next step are "perceived as highly aggressive" — 66 % of users hitting Amazon's cross-sell step showed extreme frustration — while a dismissible "Added to Cart" overlay that restates the just-added item and key order information is an accepted location ([Baymard, cart recommendations](https://baymard.com/research-articles/product-recommendations-cart); [Baymard, cross-sell benchmark](https://baymard.com/checkout-usability/benchmark/step-type/cross-sell); [Baymard, added-to-cart examples](https://www.baymard.com/ecommerce-design-examples/added-to-cart-confirmation)). The EU Consumer Rights Directive Art. 22 voids any extra payment inferred "by using default options" and entitles the consumer to a refund ([EUR-Lex 2011/83/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32011L0083)), and the CMA's 18 November 2025 guidance spells out the UK rule: **"You can't use pre-ticked boxes or other forms of automatic opt-in for optional extras"**, consent cannot come from "not changing a default option" or from being "required to tick a box to avoid paying", an unticked gift-wrap box the customer "must actively tick" is the model, and penalties reach 10 % of turnover or £300,000 ([GOV.UK, consent for additional charges](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/getting-consent-for-additional-charges-when-selling-online/getting-consent-for-additional-charges-when-selling-online)). The CMA opened investigations into eight businesses and wrote to 100 more the same month ([GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-launches-major-consumer-protection-drive-focused-on-online-pricing-practices)), and the airline precedents (Air New Zealand, Jetstar, AirAsia, Virgin Australia all forced from opt-out to opt-in) plus academic work on the "must-opt" forced-choice workaround show that regulators treat a forced decision as a grey pattern too ([Commerce Commission NZ](https://comcom.govt.nz/news-and-media/media-releases/2016/airasia-warned-over-opt-out-pricing-and-processing-fee); [AISeL, Barry/Hogan/Torres](https://aisel.aisnet.org/isd2014/proceedings2016/General/3)). Shopify's post-purchase rules encode the same ergonomics: the accept button reads "Pay now • total", the decline is visible and fixed-text, money lines are mandatory, and **at most three consecutive offers** ([Shopify post-purchase UX](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/checkout/product-offers/post-purchase/ux-guidelines)). Attach-rate numbers exist only as vendor claims — product-page checkboxes "18–25 %", post-purchase 3–8 % up to 15–25 % — with no independent head-to-head, and one CRO anecdote found a gift checkbox beside the add-to-cart button slowed sales until it was moved to the cart page (+6 %), so placement should be measured, not assumed ([EasySell](https://easysellapp.com/blogs/wiki/shopify-one-click-add-on-upsell-checkbox-aov-2026); [Bluestout](https://bluestout.com/cro-wins/one-checkbox-that-slowed-sales-and-what-fixed-it/)). Expedia's 2025 Traveler Value Index (11,000 respondents) found **complimentary add-ons the most appealing promotion for activity bookings** (48 % in the UK/AU snapshot), which argues for a free extra as the lead offer rather than a discount ([Expedia Group TVI](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/resources/blog/2025-traveler-value-index-highlights)).

### Chat assistants confirm money explicitly, cart edits implicitly, and always leave an undo

Every mature voice- or agent-commerce guideline splits confirmation by consequence. Google's conversation-design rules make explicit confirmation "rare", reserved for actions "difficult to undo, for example, deleting user data, completing a transaction", with parameters confirmed implicitly ([Google conversation design](https://developers.google.com/assistant/conversation-design/confirmations?hl=en)). Amazon's Alexa payments guidance requires an explicit "yes" before a charge, and the prompt must restate "the full details of their purchase to assure them of the final total" with tax, fees and the payment method — "Your total $6.85, including tax, charged to your card ending in 5-5-5-5. Should I place your order now?" ([Alexa Haus](https://developer.amazon.com/en-US/alexa/alexa-haus/handling-payments)). NN/g adds that dialogs should name the item and effect, use outcome-labelled buttons ("Delete file"/"Keep file") and never pre-select the confirming option, and that undo beats confirmation wherever reversal is possible ([NN/g confirmation dialogs](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). Google Pay's agentic checkout adds a price-match guard — the purchase proceeds only if the final price is the same as or lower than "the total price you confirmed" ([Google Pay help](https://support.google.com/googlepay/answer/16421147)) — and Amazon's confirmation patent describes a batch undo that reverses everything just added ([USPTO 8676669](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8676669)). The regulatory frame arrived on 9 March 2026: the CMA's guidance on AI agents states "if an AI agent you use does something illegal, you are responsible", requires agents to "properly obtain any necessary consents" and give prices "which should include all unavoidable charges", and expects "Someone with appropriate experience" to review agent responses regularly ([GOV.UK, AI agents and consumer law](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/complying-with-consumer-law-when-using-ai-agents/complying-with-consumer-law-when-using-ai-agents)). No airline or OTA chat product (FlySafair's WhatsApp bag add-on, Booking.com's Trip Planner, Expedia Romie) documents an in-chat add-on confirmation flow, so the pattern above is assembled from guidelines rather than copied from a travel peer ([Travel News, FlySafair](https://www.travelnews.co.za/node/60645); [Booking Holdings](https://www.bookingholdings.com/press-releases/booking-com-launches-new-ai-trip-planner-to-enhance-travel-planning-experience/)). This maps directly onto Trivlu's planner, whose `ApplyEditsNode` already applies ADD/REMOVE/REPLACE edits in Java against scheduling rules and returns a new generation inline: an extra is the same shape of edit with money attached, so it needs the receipt-and-confirm step the current ops do not.

## One ratio, one grade, licensed sources: building the image set

The UK grids look assembled because the crop is consistent and nothing else is. LNOF's square thumbnails mix "Black and white image of man with hand gun", "Split image of a stag do group tank in a field and someone shooting a gun" and, for adult products, "Two women dressed in their underwear performing a strip" and "A fat woman posing naked" ([LNOF Prague](https://www.lastnightoffreedom.co.uk/stag-weekends/prague/)); Pissup's adult cards are literal by filename ("stripper-on-bed.jpg", "naked-body-sushi.jpg") inside the same 270×210 frame as everything else, and one card ships with a broken image ([Pissup Prague](https://www.pissup.com/prague-stag-do/)); Chillisauce is the cleanest — a fixed 290 px fill-crop, "Popular"/"New" badges rendered in HTML rather than burned into the image, and the adult category hidden entirely ("Tooooo Spicy for Public View") ([Chillisauce Prague](https://www.chillisauce.com/stag-weekends/prague)). The marketplaces prescribe the discipline the stag sites lack. GetYourGuide's supplier guide calls the first photo "the single most important photo in your listing" and requires **horizontal 4:3, minimum 1280×960 and ideally 2048×1536**, wide shots of real participants mid-action, daylight without flash, no watermarks or text overlays, and no "Generic, AI-generated, or stock-looking photos" ([GetYourGuide Growth Hub](https://www.getyourguide.supply/articles/your-first-photo-can-make-or-break-your-bookings)). Airbnb Experiences wants vertical 2:3, "a person or people enjoying the activity", no selfies or stock, no nudity, people with alcohol who "must appear to be at or over the legal age", and permission from anyone prominently featured ([Airbnb Help 3024](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3024)). The conversion evidence is first-party and directional rather than experimental, but it all points the same way: Airbnb reports a 19–20 % booking lift from professional photography across 5,000 listings, Expedia a 63 % higher click-through for high-quality hotel photos, GetYourGuide that 55 % of travellers rank photos among the most helpful booking factors, and NN/g that users "ignore stock photos of generic people" while attending to information-carrying images and faces ([Airbnb Pro Photo](https://www.airbnb.com/professional_photography); [Expedia Group](https://welcome.expediagroup.com/en/resources/hotel-marketing-strategy-resources-tips/do-it-yourself-hotel-photography); [GetYourGuide Growth Hub](https://www.getyourguide.supply/articles/your-first-photo-can-make-or-break-your-bookings); [NN/g, photos as web content](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/photos-as-web-content/)).

Reusing competitor photos is off the table in every relevant jurisdiction. The UK IPO states plainly that "Copying images and then hosting them on another website however will usually amount to copyright infringement", that "some exceptions are not available for photographs", and that deliberate commercial-scale infringement can be criminal ([GOV.UK IPO notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet/copyright-notice-digital-images-photographs-and-the-internet)). The CJEU in *Renckhoff* held that re-posting a photo already freely available online is a new communication to the public that "requires authorisation" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/copyright/734678/copyright-and-freely-accessible-photos-the-implications-for-website-operators-and-social-media-users)), and Czech law treats photographs as works (§2(2)), gives the author the exclusive right of use (§12), and limits its personal-use exception (§30) to natural persons without commercial benefit — there is no commercial fair use ([WIPO Lex, Czech Copyright Act](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/19501)). Even under US §107 a whole photo reused to sell the same activity fails on purpose, amount and market effect ([copyright.gov](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/)). Hotlinking is no escape: the CJEU's *VG Bild-Kunst* ruling makes framing lawful only until the rightholder adds a technical anti-framing measure, Pixabay's terms forbid permanent hotlinking outright ("download them to your server first"), and a competitor can swap the file at any time ([CURIA press release](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/jcms/p1_3453439); [Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)).

The free licences differ in ways that matter for a one-off fetch-and-rehost pipeline. Unsplash grants the right "to download, copy, modify, distribute … including for commercial purposes, without permission from or attributing", but excludes "People's images if they are recognizable" and trademarks, and warrants nothing about model releases ([Unsplash licence](https://unsplash.com/license); [Unsplash terms](https://unsplash.com/terms); [Unsplash releases](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2612329-releases-and-trademark)); its API allows 50 requests an hour in demo and 1,000 in production, image-file requests do not count, and the hotlink-and-attribute rules bind only apps that serve images via the API at runtime ([Unsplash API docs](https://unsplash.com/documentation); [Unsplash API guidelines](https://help.unsplash.com/en/articles/2511245-unsplash-api-guidelines)). Pexels is free for commercial use without attribution but "Identifiable people may not appear in a bad light or in a way that is offensive", with a 200-requests-an-hour API that asks for a "prominent link to Pexels" ([Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/); [Pexels API](https://www.pexels.com/api/documentation/)). Pixabay's licence is irrevocable and royalty-free, bars use "in any immoral or illegal way, especially Content which features a recognisable person", and its API (100 requests per 60 seconds) requires downloading, 24-hour caching, and warns that webformat URLs expire after 24 hours ([Pixabay terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/); [Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)). Wikimedia Commons is CC BY or BY-SA — attribution is mandatory, **BY-SA forces the graded crop to be re-licensed share-alike**, and scripts without an informative User-Agent get a 403 — while a live API call returned a "Charles Bridge at night, Prague" file with licence, artist and attribution-required fields ready to store ([Commons reuse](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia); [WMF User-Agent policy](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Wikimedia_Foundation_User-Agent_Policy); [Commons API](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php)). Openverse is an index over Flickr and others whose `license_type=commercial` filter and ready-made `attribution` string make it the easiest CC0/BY source to automate, though its terms "disallow scraping" and cap anonymous pagination ([Openverse API](https://api.openverse.org/v1/images/); [Openverse schema](https://api.openverse.org/v1/schema/)). Freepik's free tier requires a visible "Designed by Freepik" credit next to the resource, which disqualifies it for card art ([Freepik licences](https://cn.freepik.com/ai/docs/licenses-attribution)).

For the adult categories the licence clauses and the ad platforms agree on a convention: props and venue, no recognisable face. A recognisable model captioned "strip show" is precisely the "bad light / offensive / immoral" use Pexels, Pixabay and Unsplash exclude, and no free licence warrants a release ([Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/); [Pixabay terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)). Google Ads classes strip clubs as "Sexual entertainment" (moderately restricted), lists "sexually suggestive poses or postures" and "Touching covered intimate body parts" as suggestive elements, bars all sexual-content ads from Display, YouTube and AdMob, and states that **in Germany "all sexual content won't serve"** — a direct constraint for Trivlu's `/de` market ([Google Ads policy 6023699](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6023699)). Live searches show the safe stock exists: Pexels "burlesque" returns feathered cabaret performers, a dancer in red velvet, showgirls backstage and a red feather boa; Unsplash returns neon "live music / burlesque" signs and a dancer silhouette; the tags to avoid are the ones the sites suggest alongside — erotic, lingerie, boudoir, seduction ([Pexels burlesque](https://www.pexels.com/search/burlesque/); [Unsplash burlesque](https://unsplash.com/s/photos/burlesque)). The UK sites' explicit imagery is a brand choice with no published evidence that it outperforms prop framing.

Harmonising forty mixed-source images into one set is a one-pass job. The brand-guide consensus is a single treatment — natural white balance, soft light, slight desaturation, fixed aspect ratios — so that "100 different photographs feel like they belong to the same product" ([Forrester photography](https://go.forrester.com/brand/design-system/photography/); [Frequency Media imagery](https://brand.frequency.media/guidelines/imagery); [art-direction checklist](https://openskillindex.com/skills/intense-visions-harness-engineering-design-imagery-photography-1cc7b2)). In Sharp that is `rotate()` → `resize(1200, 900, { fit: 'cover', position: 'attention' })` → `normalise({ lower: 1, upper: 99 })` → `modulate({ saturation: 0.85 })` → `linear(1.06, -6)` → a faint warm `tint` → `jpeg({ quality: 80, mozjpeg: true })`, which strips metadata by default; the ImageMagick equivalent is `-resize 1200x900^ -gravity center -extent 1200x900 -auto-level -modulate 100,85,100 -colorize 5 -quality 80` ([sharp docs](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/api-operation); [ImageMagick resize](https://usage.imagemagick.org/resize/); [ImageMagick options](https://imagemagick.org/command-line-options/)). The selection rules do more than the grade: people mid-action at a similar scale, even light, no black-and-white, no split frames, no in-frame text or logos (brands void the commercial licence on Pixabay and Unsplash), then a contact-sheet review to swap the two or three that still fight the set. The price, badges and any brand gradient belong in card CSS, as Chillisauce and Batch do, so the same JPEG serves the listing, the OG tag and ads ([Chillisauce Prague](https://www.chillisauce.com/stag-weekends/prague); [letsbatch.com](https://letsbatch.com/)).

## Recommendations, prioritised

The three uses below build on what already exists in Trivlu: the `min_price` floor (kept in sync across `BookingService`, `VoteSessionService`, `tripPricing.js` and `PlanPricer`, with `PriceQuoteService` reusing the last), the planner's `ApplyEditsNode`/`PackageEditor` edit pipeline, the `translations` column for German, the `RemoteImageFetcher` → `ImageUploadService` → R2 re-hosting path, and the `pushEvent` analytics layer. P1 items are the ones the evidence makes close to mandatory (law or strong research); P2 are differentiators; P3 are follow-ups.

## Conclusion

The market's defining choice — hide the price, name the extra, close by phone — is a legacy of the UK quote-led model, and it is now at odds with both the research and the regulators: the CMA's 2025 guidance treats hidden foreseeable charges and pre-selected extras as enforcement targets, and the marketplaces that compete for the same Prague bar crawl already print a labelled price and sell extras as options. A Prague-local seller that prices every activity and every extra with its unit, computes the group total on the page, and makes the adult disclosure block a feature rather than an embarrassment will look more trustworthy than every incumbent without inventing anything new. The deeper implication for Trivlu is that extras, group minimums and chat edits are the same problem: money attached to an itinerary line that must be explicit, totalled and reversible wherever it is touched — card, Trip Builder, vote result or planner reply — and the existing floor-in-four-places and `ApplyEditsNode` patterns are the right skeleton to hang that on.

Two things the research could not settle should be treated as experiments rather than decisions: where the extras control converts best (detail page versus added-to-trip overlay — the only evidence is vendor claims and one contrary anecdote) and whether prop-framed adult imagery sells as well as the incumbents' explicit photos (no data either way). Both are cheap to measure with the analytics layer already in place, and the second has a hard constraint that settles it for now: a single image set that must also run as a Google ad in Germany cannot contain the incumbents' imagery at all.
